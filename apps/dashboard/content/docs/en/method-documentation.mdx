The [Method chapter](/model/method) of the Model area is where you
document why each criterion exists and review it for bias, then export
that documentation as the method appendix. Approving the model itself is
the chapter after it, [Approval](/model/approval). This is compliance evidence, not a
guarantee that the model is free of bias.

## The four statuses

Each criterion moves through four statuses: "Not started", "In
progress", "Documented", and "Approved". A criterion becomes "Documented"
once its purpose, why-relevant text, bias risk, and bias comment are all
filled in; "In progress" means some but not all of those are filled in
yet. A criterion becomes "Approved" only through an explicit sign-off,
described below.

A criterion's card marks the last two of these: "Documented" with a
plain check, "Approved" with the green check the Approval checklist uses
on a passing row. The first two carry no mark at all, and the card's own
action says what is left to do: "Document" while nothing is written,
"Change" once something is.

## What you document

Opening "Document" on a criterion's card shows the library's own
definition of the criterion and two sections. Rationale holds Purpose
(what the criterion measures), Why relevant (why it matters to the
work's value and why it is gender-neutral), and Overlap with other
criteria (optional, so the same thing is not weighted twice). Bias
review holds Bias risk (Low, Medium, or High), a Bias comment (what you
considered and found), and Bias mitigation (optional, the action taken
to reduce the risk).

When two criteria you have selected are a known overlapping pair, both of
their cards carry an amber mark naming the other one, until the overlap is
noted. Writing the note on either criterion of the pair is enough; the
mark then clears on both, and so does the "Overlapping criteria are
reviewed" item on the Approval checklist. The point is that the overlap
was considered, not that it was removed.

Working conditions is not decided here. Its column on the [Criteria
chapter](/model/criteria) asks the materiality question outright and
takes one of two answers, "Yes, material" or "No, not material", each
requiring a motivation. You cannot answer "No, not material" while a
working-conditions criterion is still selected in the model. The
Approval chapter's checklist reports the decision; it is not made there
either.

## Drafting with AI

"Fill using AI" generates all six fields at once from the criterion's
name, description, and evaluation scale, without reading any personal
data. Nothing is saved until you review the draft and click "Save"
yourself; because AI text is filling the form, saving also requires
checking an acknowledgement that you have read and reviewed it first.
The button is not shown once a criterion is approved.

## Approving a criterion

A criterion is signed off from inside the same dialog: "I approve this
criterion's documentation." sits at the end of the form, and saving
records it. The box is only offered once purpose, why relevant, bias
risk, and a bias comment are filled in, and leaving it clear is a
legitimate answer, a documented draft nobody has signed off. A standing
sign-off locks the criterion's fields; clearing the box unlocks them
there and then, and saving both un-approves the criterion and reopens
the model's own approval.

## Approving the model

Once your criteria are documented, a separate step approves the model
itself: the [Approval chapter](/model/approval) runs a twelve-item checklist,
each item Required or Recommended, before "Approve model" works. The
nine Required items cover dimension coverage, the working-conditions
decision, criterion count and dimension caps, complete anchor scales,
every criterion documented and approved, a balanced weight budget, and
valid level and zone-profile rules; three Recommended items (an
unmotivated dominant dimension, an unmotivated people-leadership
weight, an unreviewed overlap) are shown too but never block approval.

Every item that is not yet satisfied carries a line under it saying what
to do and where, with a link into the chapter that owns the work. The
line is specific wherever the check knows the specifics: the dominant
dimension is named together with its share of the weighting, the
overlapping criteria are named as pairs, and the documentation item
counts the criteria still outstanding. Items that pass say nothing. Two
items name no chapter, because nothing in the app edits the level or
zone-profile rules; if either of those ever fails, it needs support.

Approving is what lets roles be rated at all (see
[Evaluating a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role)); the card then shows who
approved it and when. Any method-affecting change afterward, such as
selecting a criterion, reweighting, or changing level or zone-profile
rules, reopens approval automatically; re-approving is a fresh click
once the checklist is green again. Approving an individual criterion's
own rationale and bias review does not by itself reopen the model's
approval; the two are separate.

Every approval also stores the model exactly as it was approved.
While approval is reopened, "Restore to last approved" in the Approval
chapter puts that state back: the criteria selection, their weights and
weight motivations, each criterion's rationale, bias review and
sign-off, the working-conditions decision, and the level and
zone-profile rules. Confirming lists every change it will undo first,
including any criterion it removes together with the ratings that go
with it, and any criterion that returns, which every role then has to be
evaluated on again. Only the last approved state is kept, so a restore
always goes back to that one; the model stays open for approval
afterwards, and re-approving is the same single click.
