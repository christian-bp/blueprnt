Every employee has their own page, opened by selecting their name from the
[people register](/docs/people-register). It holds everything specific to
that person: identity, classification, and salary history. A role's own
page lists who holds it, by name, seniority, department, and FTE, but never
shows anyone's pay: salary lives only here, on the person's own page.

## Identity and classification

The page shows employee number, start date, department, FTE, and the
person's current role and seniority. Choose "Edit employee" from the actions
menu to change any of these, including the role and seniority pair; unlike a
payroll import, which never erases a field a file simply does not mention,
clearing a field here on purpose does remove its stored value. If the
seniority shown is still an unconfirmed suggestion, a hint links to
[Classifying people](/docs/classifying-people) to confirm it.

Someone who has left is archived from the same menu with "Archive employee":
the card then shows an "Archived" badge with the date, and the menu offers
"Reactivate employee" instead, plus, for an admin, "Delete employee"; an
active employee cannot be deleted without being archived first. See
[The people register](/docs/people-register) for what archiving changes.

## Salary history

A salary rail lists every recorded salary, most recent first, as a plain
list rather than a table: the year, the role and seniority that were
active at that point in time, total monthly compensation, and basic
monthly pay underneath. The role and seniority shown are the ones the
person held on that record's date, so reclassifying someone later updates
their whole salary history at once. "Add salary" records a new year's
basic monthly salary plus any additional components, such as variable pay,
bonus, or equity, each as a kind and a monthly amount; a record's own menu
can delete it, which removes it from the history and cannot be undone.

## Pay compared with the role

Once the person is classified and has at least one recorded salary, the
page shows a chart comparing their FTE-adjusted total monthly pay against
others in the same role, by seniority, colored and shaped by gender, with
the viewed person marked distinctly and a reference line at their own pay.
It needs at least one other comparable person to draw a chart at all, and a
colleague paid in a different currency is left out of the comparison, but
that exclusion is always stated as a count rather than left unmentioned.
