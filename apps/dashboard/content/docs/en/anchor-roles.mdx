An anchor role is a reference role your organization has assessed and
agreed on, used to sanity check other roles' evaluations. It is decision
support: a comparison that helps you judge whether a new result looks
right, never a replacement for rating a role against the criteria.

Do not confuse an anchor role with the anchor texts on a criterion's 1
to 5 scale (also called an "anchor" in the model). An anchor role is a
whole role your organization has designated as a reference; the
criterion's anchors are the fixed library wording that describes each of
its steps. See [Criteria and the evaluation
scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale) for the latter.

## Designating one

Designating and managing anchor roles is member-level work. From a
locked role's actions menu, choose "Designate as anchor role", set the
agreed level (one of your model's twelve), and write a motivation for
why this role is a good reference point. A role must have a locked
assessment before it can be designated, since an anchor needs its own
revealed, computed level to be useful as a reference; locking is covered
in [Evaluating a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role).

blueprnt suggests keeping 2 to 5 anchor roles in total; this is guidance
shown in the dialog, not an enforced limit.

## Status and the deviation flag

An anchor role's status is "Active", "Under review", or "Replaced"; any
member can change it from "Manage anchor role" on the role's actions
menu. A replaced anchor is never deleted, so its history stays visible.

Once designated, the role's evaluation area shows the agreed level and
motivation alongside the level blueprnt computed from the role's
evaluation. If the two differ, a deviation badge appears, flagging that
the computed level no longer matches the agreed one; the computed level
always remains the one that counts, and the deviation is a prompt to
look again, not an override.
