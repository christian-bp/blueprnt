Evaluating a role means rating it, one criterion at a time, against every
criterion in your [evaluation model](/model). blueprnt then derives the
role's weighting and level from those ratings once you lock them; neither
is ever typed in directly or overridden.

## Before you can rate

Two things need to be true first. Your organization's model needs to be
approved: until then, rating shows that the method needs approving first,
with a link for an admin to finish it on the Method page; see
[Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation). A role also needs a
complete [job profile](/docs/job-profiles), its purpose and
responsibilities filled in; open the role from [Roles](/roles), and until
its profile is complete, its evaluation area shows "Complete the job
profile first" instead of a way to rate it.

## Rating one criterion at a time

From the role, choose "Rate role" (or "Continue rating" if you already
started). Rating shows one criterion at a time, in the model's order: its
name, an assessment question, its measures and what it does not measure
as expandable context, and its fixed anchor texts at steps 1, 3, and 5
(with a considered midpoint at 2 and 4) as selectable options, plus an
always-present motivation field. A working-conditions criterion also
offers "The role is not covered by this working condition" as an option
alongside the five steps, for a role the condition simply does not apply
to. A progress line at the top reads "Criterion X of Y" so you always
know how far through you are.

A motivation is required whenever you choose step 1, 4, or 5; steps 2 and
3, and the not-covered option, do not need one. Pressing a digit key
selects the matching step, and Enter saves it and moves on, if you prefer
the keyboard to clicking. "Next criterion" saves the step you picked
before advancing; on the last criterion the same button reads "Finish
rating" instead. "Back" moves to the previous criterion without saving
anything.

To keep ratings honest, the weighting, the level, and every other
criterion's rating stay hidden throughout: each criterion is judged on its
own merits, never to hit a total. Reopening a role you started rating
resumes at the first criterion you have not yet rated.

## Locking is the reveal

Once every criterion has a rating, nothing is shown yet: "Lock assessment"
is the action that reveals the role's weighting, level, and, if your
organization has active anchor roles, how it compares to them. Locking is
explicit on purpose, so the result only ever appears once you choose to
see it. See [Weighting and levels](/docs/score-and-levels) for how these
numbers are computed and what they mean.

## Changing a rating later

A locked assessment has to be unlocked before any of its ratings can
change; unlocking hides the weighting and level again until you lock it
once more. A locked role shows "Assessed under a previous method"
whenever it was locked before your organization's current method
approval, or whenever that approval is currently reopened at all, the
common case right after any method-affecting edit; it is a prompt to
re-lock the role under the current method rather than a blocker. See
[Adjusting a rating](/docs/adjusting-a-rating) for how unlocking and
re-rating work, and when a role is blocked from either.
