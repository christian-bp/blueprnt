Equal work (lika arbete) is the pay mapping's third analysis chapter, shown in the sidebar as "Equal work", with "Equal work groups" as its fuller name. The
law requires comparing pay between women and men doing
equal work, and recording an objective reason for every difference you
cannot otherwise explain.

## Groups formed without seniority

A group is everyone sharing the same role title and the same level, and
deliberately nothing more: seniority plays no part in forming it.
Splitting by seniority as well would produce many small groups, many of
them with nobody of the other gender to compare against, and would
separate people whose only real difference is experience, which belongs in
the documented objective reason rather than in how the group is drawn.
Seniority still appears, as a column on the group's own member list,
exactly where it belongs: something that helps you judge whether
experience explains a difference, never something that removes a person
from the comparison.

## Which groups need documenting

A group enters this chapter's checklist, and carries a documentation duty,
only when it has at least one woman and one man and the women's average
pay trails the men's. A note above the checklist states how many groups
were formed in total and how many were left out, and why: some job titles
have only one person, some groups have a single gender, and in some the
women are not the ones behind. Every pay difference in a shown group is
analyzed here however small it looks, since the law sets no minimum size;
the flags below are only this tool's own way of ordering what needs
attention first, not a legal threshold.

## The finding and its flag

Opening a group states its finding in a plain sentence: how much less the
women earn on average than the men, with the group's own headcount by
gender named alongside it, or, when nothing measurable was found, that
there is no measurable pay difference between them. When the gap only
shows up in total compensation while base pay is even, the sentence names
that explicitly, rather than pointing at the smaller figure. Alongside the
finding, a flag badge reads "Under 5%", "5-10%", or "Over 10%", the tool's
own way of prioritizing groups, most severe first.

## Looking at a group's data

Above the documentation form, a summary shows both genders' headcounts and
average pay, the gap in both currency and percent, a chart plotting every
member's pay against their age or tenure so you can look for an objective
pattern, and a table listing every member by name, gender, base pay, total
compensation, and their own difference against the men's average.

## Documenting a group

A group that needs documenting is closed by choosing at least one
objective reason, from a list grouped into market reasons (such as
alternative labour market, pay level at recruitment, geographic
differentiation, or retention), individual reasons (experience, historical
pay, competence, or performance), and work reasons (responsibility), a
free-text note, or both, then choosing "Mark done and continue". Editing
the reasons or the note afterward reopens the group, and "Undo mark as
done" reopens it deliberately, so what is marked complete always matches
what is currently on file. From the same menu beside a group's heading,
or beside one person in its member table, you can also record an action
or a note without leaving the chapter; see
[Actions and notes](/docs/actions-and-notes).
