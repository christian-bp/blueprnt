Opening a pay mapping (lönekartläggning) from [Pay mappings](/pay-mappings)
lands you on its Overview, the first of the tabs across its top (Overview,
Analysis, and Actions).

## The three tiles

Three tiles lead the page. People included shows how many people this
mapping covers, with a note comparing that headcount to the mapping before
it, such as "5 people more than Pay mapping 2025", or "First pay mapping"
when there is none to compare against. Pay gap shows the unadjusted average
pay difference between women and men as a plain percentage, or "Not enough
data for an organization-level gap" when the population is too small to
measure one, again compared against the previous mapping's own figure.
Equality clock restates that same gap as time within an eight-hour working
day, in hours, minutes, and seconds, framed as how long the lower-paid
gender effectively works unpaid each day, or "No measurable pay gap" when
there is none.

## The finding sentence

Below the tiles, a sentence states the same gap in words, for example
"Women earn on average 4.1% less than men across the whole pay mapping",
beside the two gender averages themselves as labelled bars, so the reading
comes before the chart rather than after it.

## Statistics

Two expandable charts round out the page: "The whole survey", a gender
donut for the mapping's whole population with its counts and shares, and
"Gender split by pay level", a stacked bar chart across four pay
quartiles, lowest to highest, showing whether women cluster in the
lower-paid quartiles while men cluster in the upper ones.

## Everything reads from frozen data

None of these figures update as your live People or Roles change: they
are read from the snapshot taken the moment this mapping started, so they
stay exactly as they were even as your live data moves. See
[Starting a pay mapping](/docs/starting-a-pay-mapping) for what gets
frozen and why. To see where things stand today, start a new pay mapping.
