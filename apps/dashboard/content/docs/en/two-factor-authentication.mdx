Every account in blueprnt must confirm a second factor before reaching the
dashboard. There is no way to turn two-factor authentication off.

## Setting it up

The first time you sign in without a confirmed second factor, blueprnt
holds you in a setup wizard: "Secure your account", "Two-step
verification is required. Choose how you'll confirm it's you when you
sign in." You choose between two methods:

- **Authenticator app** (marked "Recommended"): use an app like Google
  Authenticator or 1Password.
- **Email codes**: blueprnt emails you a code each time you sign in.

Next, you re-enter your password ("Confirm your password", "Re-enter your
password to turn on two-step verification.") before the method is
actually turned on: enrolling a second factor always requires proving
your password again, even though you are already signed in.

For an authenticator app, you scan a QR code (or enter a key manually)
and then enter the 6-digit code it shows. For email codes, blueprnt sends
a 6-digit code to your address and you enter that instead. Either way,
the method only counts as set up once you have verified a code from it,
not merely turned on.

## Backup codes

Choosing the authenticator app also gives you a set of backup codes:
"Save these somewhere safe. Each code works once if you lose access to
your authenticator and email." You must check "I've saved my backup
codes" before finishing setup. Email codes have no separate backup codes,
since email itself is always available as a fallback.

## Every sign-in, every time

Your second factor is required on every sign-in; blueprnt never offers to
remember a device or skip the check next time.

## Managing it afterward

The "Two-step verification" card on [Account security](/account/security)
shows your current method and offers two actions:

- **Change method** restarts the setup wizard from the beginning, so you
  can switch between an authenticator app and email codes. Your current
  method keeps working only until you finish the new one; starting this
  and not finishing it partway leaves you needing to complete setup on
  your next sign-in.
- **Regenerate backup codes** (authenticator app only) issues a fresh set
  of codes, replacing the old ones. This asks for your password again,
  but does not otherwise touch your authenticator: you do not need to
  re-scan anything.

There is no way to disable two-factor authentication entirely; changing
method is the only way to switch.
