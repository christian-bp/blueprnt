Once every criterion is rated and the assessment is locked, blueprnt
computes a weighting, a level, and a zone for the role. All three are
always derived from the locked ratings and the current model; none is
ever typed in by hand. Do not confuse this weighting, a role's overall
result, with the weighting you do on the [Model](/model) area's Weighting
tab, which sets each criterion's own weight points; the two share the
word deliberately, but they are different things. See
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget) for
the latter.

## The weighting formula

For every criterion, multiply its rating (1 to 5, or 0 for a
working-conditions criterion the role is not covered by) by that
criterion's weight points, then add those results together across every
criterion. Divide that sum by the total of all the criteria's weight
points: this is the weighted mean rating. Multiply the weighted mean by
20 and round down to the nearest whole number. That final number, always
between 0 and 100 however many criteria the model has, is the weighting.
Rounding down means a role reaches a level only once its weighting has
genuinely met the threshold, never by rounding up to it.

A role's weighting only appears once its assessment is locked. Until
then, blueprnt shows how many criteria are rated instead of a partial
number, even after every criterion has one.

## Where the weighting comes from

On the role's page, once it is locked, a breakdown lists every criterion
by its contribution: a criterion's rating multiplied by its weight
points, shown as a percentage share of the total across all criteria,
sorted with the biggest driver first. This is the one number on that
breakdown that is both specific to this role and moved by reweighting the
model; it does not show the model-wide weight points, since those are
identical for every role, or the raw rating, which the breakdown already
expresses through the contribution share.

## Twelve levels in four zones

Level 1 is the highest of twelve levels, counting down from there. Your
model defines a minimum weighting for each level. The levels are grouped
into four zones, from A (levels 1 to 3) down to D (levels 10 to 12); a
zone is a broader band with a shared character, useful for talking about
where a role sits without citing its exact level.

Your organization's highest-weighted criteria, those carrying weight 4 or
5 and not a working-conditions criterion, additionally act as a profile
requirement for the top zones: a role cannot enter zone A or B on a high
total weighting alone if its ratings on those specific criteria fall
short. When that happens, the role is placed at the top of the lower
zone instead and flagged for calibration, a prompt for a person to
confirm the placement rather than an automatic override. A flat model
with every criterion at weight 3 has no profile requirement at all: no
declared priorities means no gate. A role has no level, zone, or
weighting until its assessment is locked.

## Comparing against your anchor roles

If your organization has active anchor roles, they appear alongside a
newly locked result so you can sanity check the role's level against
roles you already trust; a result that lands two or more levels from
every anchor is flagged as worth a manual check before you rely on it.
See [Anchor roles](/docs/anchor-roles) for designating and managing them.

## The result follows the model

A role's weighting, level, and zone always reflect its locked ratings and
the model as it stands right now, not as it stood when you locked it.
This means a change to the model itself, such as selecting a criterion,
changing a weight point, or reweighting, can move a role to a different
level immediately, without anyone touching its ratings. Selecting a new
criterion can even make a previously complete role incomplete again until
it is rated too. Every shift like this is recorded in the audit log. A
role locked before your model's most recent approval is marked as
assessed under a previous method until it is re-locked.

## No manual override

There is no way to set or adjust a role's level directly. If a level does
not look right, the only way to change it is to unlock the assessment and
adjust the underlying ratings (see
[Adjusting a rating](/docs/adjusting-a-rating)) or the model itself, never
the outcome. A role's level also says nothing about the seniority of
whoever holds it: seniority belongs to the person, set separately during
[Classify](/people/classify), and never affects a role's rating,
weighting, or level.
