Once every criterion is rated and the assessment is completed, blueprnt
computes a weighting and a level for the role. Both are always derived
from the completed ratings and the current model; neither is ever typed in
by hand. Do not confuse this weighting, a role's overall
result, with the weighting you do in the [Model](/model) area's
[Weighting chapter](/model/weighting), which sets each criterion's own
weight points; the two share the
word deliberately, but they are different things. See
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget) for
the latter.

## The weighting formula

For every criterion, multiply its rating (1 to 5, or 0 for a
working-conditions criterion the role is not covered by) by that
criterion's weight points, then add those results together across every
criterion. Divide that sum by the total of all the criteria's weight
points: this is the weighted mean rating. Multiply the weighted mean by
20 and round down to the nearest whole number. That final number, always
between 0 and 100 however many criteria the model has, is the weighting.
Rounding down means a role reaches a level only once its weighting has
genuinely met the threshold, never by rounding up to it.

A worked example, with four criteria and therefore a point budget of
12:

| Criterion | Weight points | Rating | Contribution |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Complexity and ambiguity | 5 | 4 | 20 |
| Autonomy and decision mandate | 3 | 5 | 15 |
| Risk and consequence | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| On-call, standby and availability requirements | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| **Total** | **12** | | **44** |

20 × 44 / 12 is 73.3, so the weighting is 73. The example shows two
things. The working-conditions 0 is a real answer, not a blank: it adds
nothing to the sum while the criterion's weight points stay in the
divisor, so a role not covered by on-call work genuinely carries less
requirement there. And the weight points decide how far one rating step
moves the result: a step on a criterion with weight v moves the
weighting by 20 × v divided by the budget, here about 8 points on the
weight-5 criterion and under 2 on the weight-1 criterion. Weight points
redistribute the same 0 to 100; they never raise the ceiling.

A role's weighting only appears once its assessment is completed. Until
then, blueprnt shows how many criteria are rated instead of a partial
number, even after every criterion has one.

## Where the weighting comes from

On the role's page, once the assessment is completed, a breakdown lists
every criterion
by its contribution: a criterion's rating multiplied by its weight
points, shown as a percentage share of the total across all criteria,
sorted with the biggest driver first. This is the one number on that
breakdown that is both specific to this role and moved by reweighting the
model; it does not show the model-wide weight points, since those are
identical for every role, or the raw rating, which the breakdown already
expresses through the contribution share.

## Twelve levels, placed with a profile check

Level 1 is the highest of twelve levels, counting down from there. Your
model defines a minimum weighting for each level. Internally, the
placement rule also groups the twelve levels into four zones, from A
(levels 1 to 3) down to D (levels 10 to 12), to apply one more check
before it settles a role's level.
[Ladder and Matrix](/docs/levels-views) show that grouping as four
bands, each naming its zone and the levels it covers.

Your organization's highest-weighted criteria, those carrying weight 4
or 5 and not a working-conditions criterion, are what that check looks
at: a role whose total weighting alone would reach the top zones' levels
still needs its ratings on those specific criteria to hold up, or the
rule settles it at the top level of the lower zone instead. A flat
model with every criterion at weight 3 has nothing for the check to
look at: no declared priorities means nothing gates the placement. A
role has no level or weighting until its assessment is completed.

## Comparing against your anchor roles

If your organization has active anchor roles, they appear alongside a
newly revealed result so you can sanity check the role's level against
roles you already trust; a result that lands two or more levels from
every anchor is flagged as worth a manual check before you rely on it.
See [Anchor roles](/docs/anchor-roles) for designating and managing them.

## The result follows the model

A role's weighting and level always reflect its completed ratings and the
model as it stands right now, not as it stood when you completed it. This
means a change to the model itself, such as selecting a criterion,
changing a weight point, or reweighting, can move a role to a different
level immediately, without anyone touching its ratings. Selecting a new
criterion can even make a previously complete role incomplete again until
it is rated too. Every shift like this is recorded in the audit log. A
completed role shows "Assessed under a previous method" whenever it was
completed before the model's current approval, or whenever the model's
approval is currently reopened at all, the common case right after any
method-affecting edit, since reopening the approval applies to every
completed role at once, not only ones that stayed completed across an
approval change. Evaluating it again under the current method clears it.

## No manual override

There is no way to set or adjust a role's level directly. If a level does
not look right, the only way to change it is to reopen the assessment and
adjust the underlying ratings (see
[Adjusting a rating](/docs/adjusting-a-rating)) or the model itself, never
the outcome. A role's level also says nothing about the seniority of
whoever holds it: seniority belongs to the person, set separately during
[Classify](/people/classify), and never affects a role's rating,
weighting, or level.
