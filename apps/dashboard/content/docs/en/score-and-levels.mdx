Once every criterion is rated, blueprnt computes two numbers for the
role: its weighting, a score from 0 to 100, and its level. Both are
always derived from the current ratings and the current model; neither
is ever typed in by hand. Do not confuse this weighting, a role's overall
result, with the weighting you do on the [Model](/model) area's Weighting
tab, which sets each criterion's own weight points; the two share the
word deliberately, but they are different things. See
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget) for
the latter.

## The weighting formula

For every criterion, multiply its rating (0 to 5) by that criterion's
weight points, then add those results together across every criterion.
Divide that sum by the total of all the criteria's weight points: this is
the weighted mean rating. Multiply the weighted mean by 20 and round down
to the nearest whole number. That final number, always between 0 and 100
however many criteria the model has, is the weighting. Rounding down
keeps the comparison against level thresholds exact.

A role's weighting only appears once every current criterion is rated.
Until then, blueprnt shows how many criteria are rated instead of a
partial number.

## Where the weighting comes from

On the role's page, once its evaluation is complete, a breakdown lists
every criterion by its contribution: a criterion's rating multiplied by
its weight points, shown as a percentage share of the total across all
criteria, sorted with the biggest driver first. This is the one number
on that breakdown that is both specific to this role and moved by
reweighting the model; it does not show the model-wide weight points,
since those are identical for every role, or the raw 0-5 rating, which
the breakdown already expresses through the contribution share.

## Levels and thresholds

Level 1 is the highest level, counting down from there. Your model
defines a threshold weighting score for each level; blueprnt assigns a
role the highest level whose threshold its weighting meets or exceeds.
A role has no level until it is complete, for the same reason it has no
weighting yet.

## Always derived, never stored

blueprnt never stores a role's weighting or level; it recomputes both,
live, every time you view them, from whatever the ratings and the model
currently are. This means a change to the model itself, such as adding a
criterion, changing a weight point, or reweighting, can move a role to a
different level immediately, without anyone touching its ratings. Adding
a criterion can even make a previously complete role incomplete again
until the new criterion is rated. Every shift like this is recorded in
the audit log.

## No manual override

There is no way to set or adjust a role's level directly. If a level
does not look right, the only way to change it is to adjust the
underlying ratings (see [Adjusting a rating](/docs/adjusting-a-rating))
or the model itself, never the outcome. A role's level also says nothing
about the seniority of whoever holds it: seniority belongs to the
person, set separately during [Classify](/people/classify), and never
affects a role's rating, weighting, or level.
