blueprnt uses a small set of terms in a precise way. Getting these five
pairs straight, and knowing that a pay mapping freezes them into a
snapshot, makes the rest of the app easier to read.

## Role versus person

A [role](/roles) describes a job, never a person: a title, a purpose, a
list of responsibilities, and the track it belongs to. A role's evaluation
never carries a name, a salary, or anyone's performance.

A [person](/people) is an employee: their name, gender, employment details,
and pay. People are imported and then assigned to a role. Many people can
be assigned to the same role over time, and a role can exist with nobody
assigned to it yet. Keeping the two apart is what lets blueprnt evaluate
the job itself, independent of who currently does it.

## Model versus evaluation

The [evaluation model](/model) is the shared measuring stick for your whole
organization: the criteria every role is judged against, and the weight
points allocated to each one. There is one model per organization, and
changing it affects every role.

[Evaluating](/docs/evaluating-a-role) a role means rating that specific
role against every criterion in the model. The rating belongs to the role,
not the model; two roles can be rated very differently against the same
model.

## Level versus seniority

A role's level is the outcome blueprnt computes from its rating: how the
role's total weighting compares to the level thresholds in your model.
Level 1 is the highest level. You never set or override a level directly;
it always follows from the rating. See the level ladder on the
[Levels](/work) page.

Seniority is different: it belongs to the person, not the role. When you
classify someone into a role, you also set their seniority within that
role's track, for example IC2 or Lead-1. A role itself never carries a
seniority, only a track; seniority is set per person from
[Classify](/people/classify).

## Step versus weight point

A step is one of the six positions, 0 to 5, on a single criterion's rating
scale. Each step has its own description, written when the criterion is
defined on the [Model](/model) page's Criteria tab, and a role's rating
against that criterion is always one of its six steps.

A weight point is different: it is how much that whole criterion counts
toward a role's total weighting, from 1 to 5, set on the
[Weighting](/model/weighting) tab. Every organization allocates the same
fixed budget, three points per criterion on average, across all its
criteria; a criterion's percent share of the total is derived from its
weight points, never entered directly.

## Role family versus track

A [role family](/docs/role-families) groups roles that share the same kind
of work, purely to organize your own register. A role belongs to at most
one family, or none.

A track is different: it is what kind of job a role is, set on the role's
job profile, and it is one of individual contributor, lead, or manager.
Neither a family nor a track ever affects a role's evaluation, weighting,
or level; two roles in the same family can sit on different tracks.

## Pay mapping as a frozen snapshot

A [pay mapping](/pay-mappings) is not a live report: starting one freezes
a snapshot that every later analysis reads from, so its numbers do not
shift after the fact. See
[Starting a pay mapping](/docs/starting-a-pay-mapping) for exactly what
gets frozen and why.
