The evaluation model is the shared measuring stick every role in your
organization is judged against: the criteria you evaluate roles on, and
how much each one counts. There is exactly one live model per
organization. Changing it (adding a criterion, rewording a step,
reallocating weight) recomputes every role's weighting and level
immediately; nothing needs a manual refresh, and there is no separate
"apply changes" step.

## The three tabs

The [Model](/model) area has three tabs: "Criteria", "Weighting", and
"Method". Each owns one part of building and maintaining the model:

- "Criteria" is where you name each criterion, describe it, and write its
  six-step evaluation scale (0 to 5). See
  [Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale).
- "Weighting" is where you decide how much each criterion counts relative
  to the others, using weight points from 1 to 5. See
  [Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget).
- "Method" is where you document why each criterion belongs in the model
  and review it for bias, then export that documentation. See
  [Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation).

A criterion's evaluation scale and its weighting are deliberately never
shown on the same screen. The 0 to 5 steps on the Criteria tab describe
how demanding a role is on that criterion; the 1 to 5 weight points on
the Weighting tab say how much that criterion matters relative to the
others. Keeping them on separate tabs is intentional: mixing them up is
the single most common source of confusion when building a model.

## One live model, always current

An organization has exactly one evaluation model at a time; there is no
history of past versions. A role's weighting and level are always
recalculated from its current ratings and the model as it stands today,
rather than fixed when the role was last evaluated. This means a model
change can move a role to a different
level without anyone touching that role directly. The audit log records
who changed the model, when, and which roles shifted level as a result,
so that change stays traceable even though the model itself keeps only
its current state.

## Getting started: the standard template

Every new organization's model is created during onboarding from the same
standard template (a full set of criteria with scales, weights, and
rationale already filled in, translated to your organization's language);
there is no choice between a template and starting from scratch at that
stage. See the [onboarding guide](/docs/onboarding-guide) for exactly how
that happens. Once onboarding is complete, the model is yours to adapt. A
criterion still carrying the ready-made text is shown translated into
whichever language you are reading the app in. The moment you edit its
wording it becomes your organization's own text, shown exactly as you
wrote it to every reader, and you can take it as far from the original as
you want.
