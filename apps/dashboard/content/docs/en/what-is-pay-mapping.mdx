A pay mapping (lönekartläggning) is the annual review Swedish law requires
every employer, whatever their size, to carry out: finding, addressing, and
preventing unjustified pay differences between women and men. The duty has
two mandatory parts each year: reviewing the pay-related rules and practice
you actually apply, and comparing pay between women and men who do equal or
equivalent work.

A separate EU pay transparency directive is also in motion, but it has not
been implemented into Swedish law yet and its content and timing remain
unsettled. It is useful context for where pay transparency rules are
heading, not the legal basis for the flow blueprnt builds today: that basis
is the Swedish Discrimination Act's existing rules.

## What blueprnt automates

Starting a pay mapping freezes a snapshot of your people, roles, and pay,
so every figure and chart afterward reads from that snapshot rather than
your live data; see
[Starting a pay mapping](/docs/starting-a-pay-mapping) for exactly what
gets frozen and why.

From there, blueprnt walks you through four chapters, each producing the
documentation the law expects, and gathers everything you decide to act on
into one plan you can follow up over time; see
[Actions and notes](/docs/actions-and-notes).

## The four chapters at a glance

1. Collaboration: a record of who took part and how, since the law
   requires employers to carry out the mapping together with employees,
   normally through the union. See [Collaboration](/docs/collaboration).
2. Rules and practice: a review of the pay policy, collective agreements,
   benefits, and pay-setting practice you apply, recording whether anything
   was found. See [Rules and practice](/docs/rules-and-practice).
3. Equal work: comparing pay between women and men doing the same job,
   grouped by what people actually do. See [Equal work](/docs/equal-work).
4. Equivalent work: comparing women-dominated groups against work valued
   as equal or lower but paid more. See
   [Equivalent work](/docs/equivalent-work).
