A criterion is one thing every role in your organization is evaluated
against, such as scope, complexity, or people responsibility. Every
criterion comes from blueprnt's controlled criteria library: you choose
which ones your organization uses in the
[Criteria chapter](/model/criteria) of the Model area, but you cannot
invent a new one, and you cannot
edit a criterion's own text.

## The criteria library and its four dimensions

The library holds 21 defined criteria, each with a fixed name,
definition, and a description of what it measures and what it does
not, already written and quality-controlled. Every criterion belongs to
exactly one of four dimensions: Competence, Effort and complexity,
Responsibility and impact, and Working conditions. The four dimensions
come from the EU pay-transparency directive and are always present;
you choose criteria within them, never the dimensions themselves.

## Selecting your organization's criteria

Your organization selects 6 to 8 criteria in total, avoiding two that
measure the same thing. The Criteria chapter gives each dimension its
own column, holding the criteria you have chosen for it. Under each
column is an "Add criterion" button that opens that dimension's library:
one row per criterion it still offers, with what it is for, whether it
is recommended for your industry, and what it would overlap with. Adding
one closes the list again.

Each dimension caps how many of its criteria you can activate at once:
up to 2 for Competence, up to 2 for Effort and complexity, up to 3 for
Responsibility and impact, and up to 1 for Working conditions. A
dimension that has reached its cap says so on the cards it is blocking,
and stops accepting a card dropped on it. Working conditions is the one
dimension you decide on rather than fill: your organization records
whether a working condition is materially relevant at all, with a
motivation either way, before the model can be approved. Removing a
selected criterion deletes every role's rating against it and
automatically redistributes its weight points across the remaining
criteria, so the model stays balanced without you rebalancing anything
by hand.

## Fixed texts, your own documentation

A criterion's name, definition, what it measures, what it does not
measure, and its assessment anchors are fixed library content, shown in
whichever language you are reading the app in; there is nothing to
write or edit on the criterion itself. What you do document, per
selected criterion, is why it belongs in your model and how it compares
to criteria you considered but did not select, in the
[Method chapter](/model/method); see
[Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation).

## The 1 to 5 assessment scale

Every criterion carries the same assessment scale, 1 to 5, with full
anchor texts at steps 1, 3, and 5 describing what a role at that step
looks like, and a considered midpoint at steps 2 and 4. The one
exception is an active working-conditions criterion, which also allows
0, meaning the role is not covered by that condition at all. The scale
is not shown while you build the model: you read it, and choose a step
from it, where someone [evaluates a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role)
against the criterion. The scale and a criterion's weight are therefore
never on the same screen: the weight sits on the criterion's own card in
the Weighting chapter and says how much the criterion counts, while the
scale
describes how demanding a role is on it; see
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget).
