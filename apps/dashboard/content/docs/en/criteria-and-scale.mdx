A criterion is one thing every role in your organization is evaluated
against, such as scope, complexity, or people responsibility. You build
and edit criteria from the "Criteria" tab of the [Model](/model) area.

## What a criterion holds

Every criterion has a Name, a Description, and an Extended description
(a longer explanation shown when someone wants more context). It also
has its evaluation scale: six texts, one for each step from 0 to 5,
describing what a role at that step looks like on this criterion. The
scale is captioned "Evaluation scale (0 to 5)" wherever it appears.

## The six-step evaluation scale

Each criterion's scale runs from step 0 (the lowest, the criterion's
weakest or absent expression) to step 5 (the highest, the strongest
reasonable expression). Write each step concretely enough that two
different people rating the same role against the same step description
would land on the same answer; a vague step invites inconsistent
ratings. This scale is what a role is actually rated against when
someone [evaluates a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role); it has nothing to do
with how much the criterion counts, which lives on the separate
Weighting tab.

## Adding a criterion

From the Criteria tab, "Add criterion" opens a form for the name,
description, extended description, and all six scale steps. A new
criterion always starts at weight 3 (the neutral weight point), so
adding one never unbalances the rest of the model; you decide whether to
change its weight afterward on the Weighting tab.

## Editing and removing a criterion

Each criterion's row menu offers "Edit" to change its name, description,
extended description, or scale text at any time; existing ratings
against that criterion are not affected. Removing a criterion deletes it
along with every role's rating against it, and automatically
redistributes its weight points across the remaining criteria so the
model stays balanced; you do not need to rebalance anything by hand
afterward.

A model needs at least five criteria. Once your organization has
finished onboarding, removal stops being available once you are down to
five: the "Remove" action becomes unavailable and a hint explains that a
model needs at least five criteria before you can remove any more.

## Why the scale and the weight are different things

The two numbers on a criterion look similar (both run on a small range)
but answer different questions. The 0 to 5 evaluation scale describes
how demanding a role is on that criterion; the 1 to 5 weight points, set
on the Weighting tab, say how much that criterion matters relative to
the other criteria in the model. A criterion can have a very demanding
top step and still carry a low weight, or the reverse. This is why the
two are never edited on the same screen: see
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget) for
how weight points work.
