A criterion is one thing every role in your organization is evaluated
against, such as scope, complexity, or people responsibility. Every
criterion comes from blueprnt's controlled criteria library: you select
which ones your organization uses from the "Criteria" tab of the
[Model](/model) area, but you cannot invent a new one, and you cannot
edit a criterion's own text.

## The criteria library and its four dimensions

The library holds 21 defined criteria, each with a fixed name,
definition, and a description of what it measures and what it does
not, already written and quality-controlled. Every criterion belongs to
exactly one of four dimensions: Competence, Effort and complexity,
Responsibility and impact, and Working conditions. The four dimensions
come from the EU pay-transparency directive and are always present;
you choose criteria within them, never the dimensions themselves.

## Selecting your organization's criteria

Your organization selects 6 to 8 criteria in total, avoiding two that
measure the same thing. Each dimension caps how many of its criteria
you can activate at once: up to 2 for Competence, up to 2 for Effort
and complexity, up to 3 for Responsibility and impact, and up to 1 for
Working conditions. Working conditions is the one dimension you decide
on rather than fill: your organization records whether a working
condition is materially relevant at all, with a motivation either way,
before the model can be approved. Deactivating a selected criterion
removes every role's rating against it and automatically redistributes
its weight points across the remaining criteria, so the model stays
balanced without you rebalancing anything by hand.

## Fixed texts, your own documentation

A criterion's name, definition, what it measures, what it does not
measure, and its assessment anchors are fixed library content, shown in
whichever language you are reading the app in; there is nothing to
write or edit on the criterion itself. What you do document, per
selected criterion, is why it belongs in your model and how it compares
to criteria you considered but did not select, on the [Model](/model)
area's Method tab; see [Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation).

## The 1 to 5 assessment scale

Every criterion carries the same assessment scale, 1 to 5, with full
anchor texts at steps 1, 3, and 5 describing what a role at that step
looks like, and a considered midpoint at steps 2 and 4. The one
exception is an active working-conditions criterion, which also allows
0, meaning the role is not covered by that condition at all. Each
criterion's card on the Criteria tab has an "Evaluation scale (1 to 5)"
section you can expand to read it as reference; the same wording is
what you actually choose a step from when someone
[evaluates a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role) against the criterion. The
scale and a criterion's weight are still never shown on the same
screen: weight lives on the separate Weighting tab and says how much
the criterion counts, while the scale, wherever it appears, describes
how demanding a role is on it; see
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget).
