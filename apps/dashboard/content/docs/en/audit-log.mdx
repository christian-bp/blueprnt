The [Audit log](/audit-log) records every change made to your
organization's data: "Every change in your organization, who made it and
when." Only organization admins can view it.

## What gets recorded

Any action that changes something in your organization writes one entry:
creating, editing, or removing a role or family, adjusting the model or
its weighting, changing an organization setting, adding or removing a
member, importing or editing people, confirming an AI weighting review,
and every step of a pay mapping. Personal account settings, such as your
own password, are deliberately not included here: those describe you,
not your organization. Your two-factor method is likewise excluded; see
[Two-factor authentication](/docs/two-factor-authentication) for how
that is managed instead.

Each entry belongs to one of seven categories: Model, Role, Organization,
Member, AI, People, and Pay. The toolbar's "Filter by category" narrows
the list to one of them, alongside a free-text search ("Search the log")
and "Filter by date" for a specific range.

## Actions that took several changes

A few things you do in the app change more than one thing at once:
correcting a criterion's documentation reopens its sign-off, rewrites the
text, and signs it off again; confirming a large classification writes an
assignment for every person. Each of those changes is still its own entry,
because the log records what actually happened. They are shown together as
one line, named after the change that best describes what you did, with a
count of how many entries it holds. Choosing that line opens them, and each
one behaves like any other entry. The count beside the panel's title, and
the pages, still count entries rather than actions. A very large
confirmation therefore fills more than one page, and each page shows the
part of it that page holds.

## The detail sheet

Choosing "View details" on an entry opens its full record: who made the
change ("Who"), when ("When"), which category it falls under
("Category"), and what changed ("What changed"). Where a field has a
clear before-and-after value, such as a renamed role or an adjusted
weight, it renders as an arrow from the old value to the new one. Where
an entry instead represents a batch of activity, such as an import, it
shows counts instead, like how many rows were created versus updated.

## People and erasure

An employee's identity fields (name, gender, employee number, birth
date, and job title) appear in the audit log just like any other
change, so that correcting one of them is traceable. If that employee is
later erased, those identity values are replaced with an "Erased" marker
everywhere they appeared in the log; the fact that a change happened, and
when, remains. See [GDPR and erasure](/docs/gdpr-and-erasure) for what
this does and does not mean.
