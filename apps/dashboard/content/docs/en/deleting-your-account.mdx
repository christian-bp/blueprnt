Deleting your account is the GDPR right to erasure applied to your own
user identity, not an archive: once confirmed, it happens immediately and
cannot be undone or reversed afterward.

## Where to find it

The "Delete account" card sits by itself in the Danger zone of
[Account security](/account/security): "Permanently delete your account
and all associated data. This cannot be undone."

## Confirming the deletion

Opening it asks for two things before the delete button turns on: typing
your own email address (case does not matter), and your current
password. Typing anything else for the email simply leaves the button
disabled rather than showing an error. Choose "Delete my account" once
both are filled in correctly.

## What gets erased

Deleting your account removes your identity everywhere it lives: your
sign-in credentials, your sessions, your membership in every organization
you belong to, and any pending invitations addressed to you. Your avatar
image is deleted as well, along with every assistant conversation you
have, across every organization you belong to. Earlier audit log entries
you authored keep a record that an action happened, but your name on
them is replaced with a generic "Deleted user" marker, the same approach
used when [an employee is erased](/docs/erasing-a-person).

Deleting your own account only removes your personal identity. It never
deletes your organization, its roles, its people, or anything else that
belongs to the organization.

## If you are the last administrator

If you are the only administrator of an organization, the delete option
is replaced entirely by a note asking you to contact support instead: an
organization can never be left with zero administrators. Promote another
member to admin first, or reach out to support, before you can delete
your account.
