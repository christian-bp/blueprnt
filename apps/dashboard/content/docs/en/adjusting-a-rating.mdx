---
title: Adjusting a rating
description: Re-entering the rating stepper to change a criterion's rating, and what changes are recorded.
section: evaluation
---

A role's ratings are never final. As long as the role is not archived,
you can revisit any criterion and change what you rated it, and blueprnt
recomputes the weighting and level immediately from the new ratings.

## Reopening the stepper

From a fully evaluated role's actions menu, choose "Adjust ratings". This
opens the same stepper described in
[Evaluating a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role), starting again at the first
criterion, but pre-filled: every criterion already shows the step you
picked and any motivation you wrote, so you are reviewing and editing
your existing judgment rather than starting from nothing. The weighting
and level stay hidden while you step through, exactly as they do the
first time; you only see the result again once you reach the end.

Changing a step and choosing "Next criterion" saves that criterion
immediately, one at a time, the same as during the first rating.
Submitting the same step and motivation that are already there changes
nothing, and no audit entry is created.

## When adjusting is blocked

Once a role is archived, it can no longer be rated or adjusted at all.
"Adjust ratings" no longer appears on its menu, and archiving is
one-way, so there is no way to reopen an archived role for further
rating; see [Archiving a role](/docs/archiving-roles) for what else
becomes read-only. Rating also still requires a complete job profile, the
same precondition as the first time; this is normally already satisfied
by the time a role has a full evaluation to adjust.

## What gets logged

Every saved change to a rating is recorded in the audit log as "Rating
changed", with the criterion's before and after value and motivation. If
the change moves the role to a different level, blueprnt also logs a
separate "Level recalibrated" entry pointing back to the rating that
caused it, so the history of why a role's level moved stays visible
alongside the ratings themselves.
