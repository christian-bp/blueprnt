title: Glossary

---

blueprnt uses a small set of terms precisely, on purpose. This page collects
every one of them in one place, each with a short definition and, where a
neighbouring term is easy to confuse it with, the line between the two.

## Action

An action is a follow-up record in a pay mapping: an issue, a planned
action, an optional objective reason, a responsible person, a planned date,
and a status of Not started, In progress, or Done.

Use an action when something needs to be done about a finding; use a note
instead when nothing does. See [Actions and notes](/docs/actions-and-notes).

## AI suggestion

An AI suggestion is an AI-proposed value, such as a job profile draft or a
weighting review, always labelled as AI-generated, showing where it came
from and who confirmed it, and carrying a status of proposed, confirmed, or
rejected.

HR always reviews and confirms a suggestion before it takes effect; AI never
decides on its own and never touches a role's weighting or level directly.
See [How AI is used](/docs/how-ai-is-used).

## Anchor

An anchor is the fixed library text describing what a criterion's
assessment scale means at one of its steps: step 1, 3, and 5 always, with a
considered midpoint at 2 and 4.

Do not confuse an anchor with an anchor role: an anchor is fixed wording on
a criterion's scale, an anchor role is a whole reference role your
organization designates. See
[Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale).

## Anchor role

An anchor role is a reference role your organization has evaluated,
locked, and agreed on, used to sanity check other roles' results after
they are rated.

It is decision support, never a replacement for rating a role against the
criteria, and the shared word is a coincidence. See
[Anchor roles](/docs/anchor-roles).

## Assessment

An assessment is the record of evaluating a role against the model: its
ratings, and any motivations. While it is open, no result exists anywhere;
locking it is what reveals the role's computed weighting, level, and zone.
Unlocking to change a rating is explicit, and hides the result again until
the assessment is locked once more.

See [Evaluating a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role).

## Audit log

The audit log is the traceable record of changes to your organization's
data: who made a change, when, and what changed.

See [Audit log](/docs/audit-log).

## Bias review

A bias review is the per-criterion gender and bias risk assessment you
document in the Model area's Method chapter: a risk level, a comment, and any mitigation
taken.

See [Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation).

## Classification

Classification is assigning an imported employee to a role and a seniority
within that role's track, from a suggestion blueprnt works out from your
existing data and you confirm.

Nothing is ever classified automatically, and the seniority it sets belongs
to the person, never the role. See [Classifying people](/docs/classifying-people).

## Collaboration

Collaboration (samverkan) is the pay mapping's first analysis chapter: a
record of who took part and how, since Swedish law requires carrying out a
pay mapping together with employees.

See [Collaboration](/docs/collaboration).

## Criterion

A criterion is one thing every role in your organization is evaluated
against, such as scope or complexity, with its own 1 to 5 assessment scale.
Every criterion comes from blueprnt's controlled library of 21; your
organization selects which ones to use, but cannot write a new one or edit
a selected one's text.

See [Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale).

## Criterion rationale

Criterion rationale is the documented justification for a criterion: its
purpose, why it matters to the work's value, and any overlap noted with
other criteria, and, once documented, who approved it.

Rationale answers why the criterion belongs in the model; bias review
answers what could skew it and what you do about that. See
[Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation).

## Dimension

A dimension is one of the four fixed categories every criterion belongs
to: Competence, Effort and complexity, Responsibility and impact, and
Working conditions. Dimensions are always present and never chosen; your
organization selects criteria within them, capped per dimension.

See [Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale).

## Equal work

Equal work (lika arbete) compares pay between women and men in the same
comparison group, grouped by role title and level, without seniority.

Equal work compares people doing the same job; equivalent work instead
compares a women-dominated group against different work valued the same or
lower. See [Equal work](/docs/equal-work).

## Equivalent work

Equivalent work (likvärdigt arbete) compares a women-dominated group's pay
against other work the evaluation model values as equal or lower, but that
still pays more.

See [Equivalent work](/docs/equivalent-work) for the comparison it
complements rather than replaces.

## Frozen data

Frozen data is the part of a pay mapping that can never change once the
mapping is started: salaries, role level and track, demographics, and
ratings, together with the complete method evidence that produced them
(criteria with their dimensions and weights, level and zone rules, the
materiality decision, and approval metadata), all captured at the
reference date.

Your working material stays editable, comparison groups, notes and actions,
but the frozen figures stay exactly as they were even as your live
organization moves on. See
[The pay mapping overview](/docs/pay-mapping-overview).

## Job profile

A job profile is the standardized description a role needs before it can be
evaluated: its identity, purpose, and responsibilities.

See [Job profiles](/docs/job-profiles).

## Level

A role's level is the outcome blueprnt computes from its weighting: how
that weighting compares to the level thresholds in your model, among
twelve levels grouped into four zones. Level 1 is the highest level.

Never confuse level with seniority: level is the role's computed, org-wide
outcome, while seniority belongs to the person and never affects it. See
[Weighting and levels](/docs/score-and-levels).

## Level threshold

A level threshold is the minimum weighting, on the normalized 0 to 100
scale, that a role must reach to land in a given level, one of twelve.

See [Weighting and levels](/docs/score-and-levels).

## Materiality decision

The materiality decision is your organization's recorded answer to
whether a working-conditions criterion is active in your model or, with a
motivation, tested and found not materially relevant. It is required
before the model can be approved.

See [Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale).

## Method appendix

The method appendix is the exportable document collecting your model's
criteria, weight points and shares, criterion rationale, and bias review.

See [The method appendix PDF](/docs/method-appendix-pdf).

## Model approval

Model approval is the status confirming your model's method has passed
its checklist and is ready to rate roles against. A method-affecting
change, such as selecting a criterion or reweighting, reopens approval for
a fresh one-click confirmation.

See [Method documentation](/docs/method-documentation).

## Motivation

A motivation is a short free-text explanation for a rating, required when
you rate a criterion 1, 4, or 5, and optional otherwise.

See [Evaluating a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role).

## Note

A note is a free-text record attached to a finding, with one of three
types: objective reason documented, further discussion needed, or no action
needed.

Use a note when nothing needs to be acted on; use an action instead when
something does. See [Actions and notes](/docs/actions-and-notes).

## Pay gap

The pay gap is the unadjusted average pay difference between women and men,
shown as a plain percentage in a pay mapping's overview.

See [The pay mapping overview](/docs/pay-mapping-overview).

## Pay mapping

A pay mapping (lönekartläggning) freezes a snapshot of pay, roles, and
demographics, then analyzes that snapshot for unjustified pay
differences between women and men.

See [What is a pay mapping](/docs/what-is-pay-mapping) for the
underlying Swedish duty and its four chapters.

## Point budget

The point budget is the total number of weight points to distribute across
a model's criteria: criteria count times three, exactly.

See [Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget).

## Quartile

A quartile is one of four pay-level groupings, lowest to highest, used in a
pay mapping's gender split chart to show whether women and men cluster at
different pay levels.

See [The pay mapping overview](/docs/pay-mapping-overview).

## Reference date

The reference date is the day a pay mapping's snapshot was frozen: the
moment you started that mapping, set automatically to that day and never
chosen by hand.

See [Starting a pay mapping](/docs/starting-a-pay-mapping).

## Role

A role is a job or position evaluated by its content, purpose, and
responsibilities, never by the person currently holding it.

A role never carries a name, a salary, or anyone's performance; that
belongs to a person, a separate record entirely. See
[Key concepts](/docs/key-concepts).

## Role family

A role family groups roles that share the same kind of work, purely for
organizing your register.

Do not confuse a family with a track: a track is what kind of job a role
is, a family is how you choose to group related roles. Neither affects a
role's evaluation, weighting, or level. See
[Role families](/docs/role-families).

## Rules and practice

Rules and practice (bestämmelser och praxis) is the pay mapping's second
analysis chapter: a review of the pay policy, agreements, and pay-setting
practice you actually apply.

See [Rules and practice](/docs/rules-and-practice).

## Seniority

Seniority is an employee's seniority within their role's track, for example
IC2 or Lead-1, set on the person during classification.

Seniority never sits on the role and never affects that role's rating,
weighting, or level; only the person carries it. See
[Classifying people](/docs/classifying-people).

## Share

A criterion's share is its derived percent weight: its weight points
divided by the sum of all weight points.

A share is always calculated, never entered directly; it moves
automatically whenever a weight point changes. See
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget).

## Step

A step is one of the five positions, 1 to 5, on a criterion's assessment
scale, each with its own fixed anchor text at 1, 3, and 5. The value 0
exists only for an active working-conditions criterion, meaning the role
is not covered by it at all.

Do not confuse a step with a weight point: a step describes how demanding a
role is on a criterion, a weight point says how much that criterion counts.
See [Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale).

## Template

A template was the ready-made model a new organization used to start from:
criteria, anchor texts, and weight points, already filled in. blueprnt no
longer has one. A new organization now starts from an empty model and
builds it by selecting from the criteria library instead.

See [Model overview](/docs/model-overview).

## Track

A track is what kind of job a role is: Individual contributor, Lead, or
Manager.

A track describes the role, never the person, and it is not the same as a
role family, which is how you group roles for your own register. See
[Job profiles](/docs/job-profiles).

## Weight points

Weight points are a criterion's weight, a whole number from 1 to 5, set by
HR under the model's fixed point budget.

Do not confuse weight points with the 1 to 5 assessment scale: weight
points say how much a criterion counts, the scale describes how demanding a
role is on it. See
[Weighting and the point budget](/docs/weighting-and-point-budget).

## Weighting

Weighting is a role's weighted total: a score from 0 to 100, rounded down
to a whole number, computed from its ratings and each criterion's weight
points.

The same word also names the process of allocating weight points to
criteria in the Model area's Weighting chapter. The two are different things:
this one is a result, that one is a task. See
[Weighting and levels](/docs/score-and-levels).

## Zone

A zone is one of four internal groupings of neighbouring levels that
blueprnt's placement rule uses, from A (levels 1 to 3, the
highest-valued work) to D (levels 10 to 12). Your organization's
highest-weighted criteria act as a profile requirement inside that
rule, so a role cannot reach a top zone's levels on a high total
weighting alone. You see the effect on the level itself; there is no
separate zone label anywhere in the app.

See [Weighting and levels](/docs/score-and-levels).
