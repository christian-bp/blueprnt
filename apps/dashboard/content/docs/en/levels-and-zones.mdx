A role's weighting decides its level, and the twelve levels are grouped
into four zones. The zone is not a second judgement and is never rated: it
is the qualitative character several neighbouring levels share, and the
place the model's own priorities are allowed to speak. Level 1 is the
highest.

## The four zones

Every zone spans exactly three levels: A is levels 1 to 3, B is 4 to 6,
C is 7 to 9, and D is 10 to 12. That mapping is fixed and is never
configured.

### Zone A: Strategic leadership

Company-wide and strategic roles. Shapes the company's long-term direction,
its critical choices or its overall ability to succeed.

**Typically here:** Company-leading roles, business-critical leading
experts, or roles with company-wide responsibility.

### Zone B: Broad-impact leaders

Leading specialist and manager roles with broad impact. Has broad, lasting
and often cross-functional impact on a material part of the business.

**Typically here:** Function-leading roles, senior managers and leading
specialists with a substantial mandate or responsibility for consequences.

### Zone C: Independent specialists

Independent specialist and operational leadership roles. Has independent
responsibility for a clearly defined area, advanced work requirements, or
impact on teams and adjacent functions.

**Typically here:** Senior specialists, team managers, operational leaders
and roles with established professional responsibility.

### Zone D: Professional core

Professional and supporting roles with more limited impact. Has clear and
relevant requirements, but normally a more limited reach, lower decision-
making authority or more established frames.

**Typically here:** Professional roles, coordinating roles, administrative
support and operational roles.

## Where a role sits inside its zone

The three levels inside a zone are not three kinds of job. They are a
successive difference in requirement profile within one broad character.

- **Entry to the zone.** The role meets the zone's basic qualitative requirements and profile requirements.
- **Established zone profile.** The role meets the zone's requirements clearly and has a stable profile that is typical for the zone.
- **Upper part of the zone.** The role sits close to the next zone through greater reach, complexity, responsibility or consequence, but does not yet meet the next zone's overall profile.

Zone A's top level is the exception, because no zone stands above it:
The role sits at the top of the architecture. No zone stands above it, and
its reach, complexity, responsibility and consequence are the greatest the
model describes.

## The profile requirement

A high total alone must not buy a high zone. Your model's **profile
criteria** are the ones you weighted 4 or 5, the requirements you declared
most value-driving, and a zone can demand a minimum step on every one of
them: zone A demands step 4 on every profile criterion, zone B step 3, and
zones C and D none. A working-conditions criterion is never a profile
criterion whatever its weight: its 0 means the role is not covered by the
condition, not that the requirement is low, so gating a zone on it would cap
every role that is simply not exposed.

The requirement can only ever lower a placement, never lift one. A role
whose weighting reaches a zone but whose profile does not is held at the
top level of the zone below, and flagged so a person looks at it. A model
that weighted everything the same has no profile criteria at all, and so no
profile requirement to meet.

## The thresholds themselves

Each of the twelve levels opens at a weighting, and a role lands on the
highest level whose opening it reaches. The ladder is progressive: the gap
between one level's opening and the next widens toward the top, so climbing
into the highest levels takes a successively larger change in the role's
whole weighting.

The numbers are the method's own and are the same in every organization:
nothing in the app edits them, so a level means the same thing wherever it
is read. They are printed in full in
[the method appendix](/docs/method-appendix-pdf), the document that is the
evidence of what your model measures.
