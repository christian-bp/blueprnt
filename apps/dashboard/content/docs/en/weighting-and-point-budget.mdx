Weighting is where you decide how much each criterion counts relative to
the others, and it is the second chapter of the [Model](/model) area,
[Weighting](/model/weighting): every criterion you have chosen carries
its own five-button weight row on the card it sits in, "Save weighting"
sits on the chapter's top row, and a small pill floats above the foot
of the screen with the running budget while the total does not balance. This is separate from
the criterion's evaluation scale (see [Criteria and the evaluation
scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale)); weighting is never a verdict on
whether a criterion is good, it is how much influence it has on a role's
weighting and, all else equal, relative pay.

## Weight points

Each criterion gets a whole number of weight points from 1 to 5. Three
is the neutral weight point, meaning clear and balanced influence; 1 is
very low influence and 5 is very strong influence. Every new criterion
starts at 3.

## The point budget

The total number of weight points to distribute is fixed by how many
criteria the model has: three points per criterion. A model with seven
criteria has a budget of 21 points; add an eighth criterion and the
budget becomes 24. The sum of every criterion's weight points must equal
the budget exactly, no more and no less. There is no free-percentage or
custom-number option: weighting always works this way.

## Zero-sum rebalancing

Changing one criterion's weight point stays an unsaved draft until you
save; nothing changes for anyone else until then. While you work, the
pill tracks it: a count of how many points are still left to distribute,
or how many are over the budget, while the total does not match, and
nothing at all once the allocation adds up, since a budget that balances
needs no announcement. "Save weighting" appears on the chapter's top row
once the budget balances and something is unsaved. Raising one
criterion's weight always means lowering others to stay on budget.

## Derived shares

Next to each criterion you also see its percent share of the total
weight, labelled "of the weight". This share is always calculated from
the weight points, never entered directly; it moves automatically
whenever any criterion's weight point changes.

## Saving the whole allocation at once

"Save weighting", on the chapter's top row, saves every criterion's
weight point in one go. The allocation you see is what you get: it is
never saved half-finished, so the model is never left off budget. The
button is only offered once the budget is exactly balanced and there is
something to record; with a balanced allocation that is already saved it
goes away again, so a save that would change nothing cannot happen and
never reaches the audit log. A precondition
worth knowing: you cannot save a partial or over-budget allocation, the
whole model has to balance first.

## Motivating a heavy weight

Next to each dimension's name you also see that dimension's own share of
the weighting. When one dimension carries more than 40% of it, a note
appears under that heading saying so, and asks you to record why, or to
move weight points to another dimension. Either answer is fine; what the
method does not accept is a dominant dimension nobody decided on.

"Motivate the weighting" opens a field for that reason. It is saved on
the heaviest criterion in the dimension, which the dialog names, and it
goes into the method appendix beside that criterion. Once it is
recorded, the note drops its warning colour and keeps an "Edit the
motivation" button, so the text stays reachable where the weighting is
done.

A people-leadership criterion weighted 4 or 5 gets a note of its own, on
the same terms, because a heavy weight on managing people is the single
most common way a model quietly favours one career path. Its motivation
is saved on that criterion itself. Both notes are the same two items the
[Approval chapter](/model/approval) checklist reports as "No dimension
dominates the weighting unexplained" and "People leadership's weight is
motivated"; recording the reason here is what clears them there.
