The "Weighting" tab of the [Model](/model) area is where you decide how
much each criterion counts relative to the others. This is separate from
the criterion's evaluation scale (see
[Criteria and the evaluation scale](/docs/criteria-and-scale)); weighting
is never a verdict on whether a criterion is good, it is how much
influence it has on a role's weighting and, all else equal, relative
pay.

## Weight points

Each criterion gets a whole number of weight points from 1 to 5. Three
is the neutral weight point, meaning clear and balanced influence; 1 is
very low influence and 5 is very strong influence. Every new criterion
starts at 3.

## The point budget

The total number of weight points to distribute is fixed by how many
criteria the model has: three points per criterion. A model with nine
criteria has a budget of 27 points; add a tenth criterion and the budget
becomes 30. The sum of every criterion's weight points must equal the
budget exactly, no more and no less. There is no free-percentage or
custom-number option: weighting always works this way, even for a fully
custom model.

## Zero-sum rebalancing

Changing one criterion's weight point on the Weighting tab is a local
draft until you save; nothing is written until then. While you work, a
status line at the top of the tab tracks the budget: "All weight points
are distributed" once the total matches the budget exactly, or a count of
how many points are still left to distribute, or how many are over the
budget, while it does not. Raising one criterion's weight always means
lowering others to stay on budget.

## Derived shares

Next to each criterion you also see its percent share of the total
weight, labelled "of the total weight". This share is always calculated
from the weight points, never entered directly; it moves automatically
whenever any criterion's weight point changes.

## Saving atomically

"Save weighting" saves the whole allocation at once: every criterion's
weight point is written together, or none of them are. The button is
only enabled once the budget is exactly balanced and something has
actually changed; saving an allocation that matches what is already
stored does nothing and does not appear in the audit log. A precondition
worth knowing: you cannot save a partial or over-budget allocation, the
tab requires the whole model to balance first.
