blueprnt supports the GDPR right to erasure with a true, permanent
deletion, never a soft "deactivated" flag. There are two separate paths,
for two different kinds of person.

## Erasing an employee

An employee is a person recorded in your organization's people register,
not a user of the app. Any member erases one from
[Erasing a person](/docs/erasing-a-person): it permanently deletes that
person's record together with their salary records and role assignments.

## Erasing your own account

You are a signed-in user of blueprnt, separate from any employee record.
Deleting your own account, described in [Deleting your
account](/docs/deleting-your-account), permanently removes your sign-in
identity, your sessions, your organization memberships, and your
assistant conversations. It never touches your organization's employee
data.

## What stays in the audit trail, and why

Either kind of erasure keeps the audit log itself: the record that a
change happened, and when, has lasting value for accountability, so it
is not deleted along with the person. What erasure does instead is blank
out the identifying details inside it. An erased employee's name,
gender, employee number, birth date, and job title are replaced with an
"Erased" marker everywhere they appear in earlier log entries; an erased
user's name on entries they authored becomes a generic "Deleted user"
marker.

This is pseudonymization, not the disappearance of every trace. Entries
about an erased employee still keep structural details that are not
identity, such as department, country, or employment type, alongside the
timestamp of each change. In a very small organization, someone with
inside knowledge could still piece together who a row was likely about
from those details. When you describe this to employees or a works
council, avoid saying that erasure leaves no personal data behind. The
accurate claim is that no direct identifier survives it, and what
remains is kept on the basis that it documents a change to an
organizational record, not to identify anyone.

## Data stays minimal by design

blueprnt keeps personal data out of places that do not need it in the
first place. A role describes a job, never a person: role, evaluation
model, and AI-related records never carry an individual's name, salary,
or performance. Employee data lives only in the people register and its
directly related records, so erasure has a small, well-defined set of
places to reach.
