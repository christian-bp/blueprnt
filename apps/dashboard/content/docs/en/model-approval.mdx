[Approval](/model/approval) is the model's last chapter, and the one that
makes rating possible: no role can be evaluated against a method nobody
has approved. The chapter renders three things in order. What approving
would change comes first, when there is anything to say. Then the method
appendix, the document that is the evidence of what approving certifies.
Then the approval card itself, with its ten checks.

## What approving would change

When the model has been approved before and approving it again would move
roles to other levels, the Approval chapter opens with "What approving this
would change", above the checklist. It compares the placements you have now
against the method you last approved: how many completed placements would move,
how many roles sit in each zone on either side, which roles would move and
from which level to which, and the same movement summarized per role family
and per gender make-up (women-dominated, men-dominated, mixed, and roles with
nobody assigned, counted only). If more roles move than the list shows, it
says how many more.

The panel appears only when it has something to say. A model that has never
been approved has nothing to compare against, and a change that moves nobody
is not reported. It moves roles, not people: no pay changes here.

Not every consequence is a move between levels. If the method has gained a
criterion, every role whose assessment was already completed has no rating for it, so it
cannot be placed at all until someone rates it: the panel says how many roles
would lose their level, and each one appears in the list reading "From level 6
to no level". The mirror happens too, where a role that could not be placed
under the approved method can be placed now. Those two are counted separately
from the roles that simply move to another level, so the three figures never
describe the same role twice.

The zone table counts completed placements only, the same roles the rest of the
panel is about.

## The method appendix

Above the approval card, the method appendix has a card of its own: the
document's name, whether it currently reads DRAFT or FINAL, and
"Download as PDF". It sits here rather than on another chapter because
the appendix is the evidence of exactly what approving certifies, and it
is worth reading before you certify it. What the document contains is on
[The method appendix PDF](/docs/method-appendix-pdf).

## Approving the model

Once your criteria are documented, a separate step approves the model
itself: the [Approval chapter](/model/approval) runs a ten-item
checklist in two groups, Required checks and Recommended, before
"Approve model" works. The seven Required items cover dimension coverage,
the working-conditions decision, criterion count and dimension caps,
complete anchor scales, every criterion documented and approved, and a
balanced weight budget; three Recommended items (an unmotivated dominant
dimension, an unmotivated people-leadership weight, an unreviewed
overlap) are shown too but never block approval.

Every item that is not yet satisfied carries a line under it saying what
to do and where, with a link into the chapter that owns the work. The
line is specific wherever the check knows the specifics: the dominant
dimension is named together with its share of the weighting, the
overlapping criteria are named as pairs, and the documentation item
counts the criteria still outstanding. Items that pass say nothing.

Approving is what lets roles be rated at all (see
[Evaluating a role](/docs/evaluating-a-role)); the card then shows who
approved it and when. Any method-affecting change afterward, such as
selecting a criterion, reweighting, or changing the working-conditions
decision, reopens approval automatically; re-approving is a fresh click
once the checklist is green again. Approving an individual criterion's
own rationale and bias review does not by itself reopen the model's
approval; the two are separate.

Every approval also stores the model exactly as it was approved.
While approval is reopened, and the model actually differs from that
stored state, "Restore to last approved" in the Approval
chapter puts that state back: the criteria selection, their weights and
weight motivations, each criterion's rationale, bias review and
sign-off, and the working-conditions decision. Confirming lists every
change it will undo first, including any criterion it removes together with the ratings that go
with it, and any criterion that returns, which every role then has to be
evaluated on again. Only the last approved state is kept, so a restore
always goes back to that one; the model stays open for approval
afterwards, and re-approving is the same single click.
